Structured data helps search. It doesn't guarantee AI citations.

Lorenz Espinosa June 14, 2026 Revised October 6, 2026

Structured data gives a search engine clues about a page. Google says it can enable richer search results. That is useful, but adding JSON-LD does not guarantee an AI citation. For Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, the usual Search fundamentals still apply. There is no special schema required for those features. [1] [2]

What structured data is good for

Markup can describe what's on a page, such as an article or the person who wrote it. Google uses it to understand content and to make certain rich results possible. Keep it consistent with what a reader can see. A block of markup cannot make an unsupported claim credible. [1] [2]

Direct answer
Does schema markup make an AI system cite your page?

No provider guidance cited here makes that promise. Google says a page must be indexed and eligible for a Search snippet to appear as a supporting link in its AI features. Meeting those requirements still does not guarantee that the page will be shown. [1]

Different products, different controls

Google documents its own AI features, not the citation rules for every assistant. OpenAI separately documents OAI-SearchBot for ChatGPT Search and GPTBot for model training; site owners can set different crawler permissions for each. Allowing a crawler is a technical choice. It is not proof that a particular answer will cite your site. [1] [3]

What I can show on this site

The canonical URLs, article text and structured data are public. You can check whether the markup matches the page. I also keep llms.txt as a short site summary. None of those files proves a change in citations. Google says new AI text files or special markup are not required to appear in its AI Overviews or AI Mode. [1]

How I'd check whether changes help

Start with a useful question the page answers. Record the query, the page shown, the date and any referral visits you can measure. Google includes traffic from its AI features in Search Console's overall Web search reports. That is useful context, but it is not a promise that any one edit caused an AI system to cite you. [1]

My rule is simpler than a GEO checklist: publish accurate pages that people can read and reach, then add relevant markup that agrees with them. Measure what happens. If you cannot show a result, do not claim one.
