Workflow design AI where useful | Revised October 6, 2026

When AI belongs in a workflow.

Some steps need language interpretation. Others follow a clear rule. A good workflow makes that distinction before adding a model.

Lorenz Leslie Espinosa IT Systems & Automation Consultant

A workflow usually has several jobs hidden inside it: collect information, check it, move it, and decide what needs a person. Putting AI over the whole chain makes it harder to tell which step failed. I would start by drawing the handoff.

Let rules handle the parts that are rules

If a form requires an email address or a record must go to a named system, write that requirement down and test it. What happens with a missing field, a duplicate, or a connected service that does not answer? Those questions matter whether AI is involved or not.

Rules do not make a workflow automatically reliable. They make the expected behavior easier to inspect. A failed request still needs an owner and a path for review.

Give AI one defined task

Free-form text can be useful input for a summary or draft. Before adding a model, decide what information it can see, what kind of output it should return, and who reviews that output. If the step could affect a record or a message to another person, make the approval point explicit.

The answer may be to leave AI out. That is a design decision, not a shortcoming.

Test the whole handoff

Try an ordinary request, then one with missing information. Check what a user sees when a connected tool is down. If a model is used, review examples where it gets the context wrong or gives an answer that sounds plausible but should not be accepted.

Measure the cost and time of the complete task, including retries and review. The right choice depends on the workflow and the team that will maintain it. Vendor prices can change, so use current terms when you do the comparison rather than an old multiplier from a blog post.

Leave someone a way to operate it

Document which system owns the record, where errors appear, and who handles them. A workflow is easier to support when people can tell whether it finished, is waiting for review, or needs to be retried.
