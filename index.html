Good candidates

Repetitive, important work that is difficult to track by hand.

The answer may be process improvement, integration, automation, or a simpler correction. The work starts with the real process—not a preferred platform.

01

New inquiries and intake

Capture information once, send it to the right person or system, confirm receipt, and make the next follow-up visible.

02

Documents and status updates

Track what is still needed, who owns the next step, and when another person or system needs an update.

03

Approvals and reconciliation

Compare records, flag mismatches, and ask a person before decisions or changes that should not happen automatically.

Safeguards and deliverables

Know what can go wrong, what you receive, and who owns it afterward.

Before launch, define access, confidential-information handling, human approval, tests, useful logs, alerts, recovery, operating instructions, and support. This is systems design, not legal advice or a compliance guarantee.

Workflow and systems review

A documented process map, gaps and unusual cases, practical options, and a recommended next step—including when automation is not the answer.

Integration and automation build

Approved connections, validation, manual approvals, recorded test results, alerts, recovery instructions, and an operating guide.

Monitoring and support

Agreed checks, alert recipients, change handling, maintenance, and defined support responsibilities. Support is scoped, not implied to be unlimited.

Professional background

Automation informed by practical IT systems experience.

My background includes IT support, systems administration, infrastructure, network troubleshooting, application support, APIs, and operational documentation. That experience shapes how I approach automation: understand the environment, reduce avoidable risk, test the real workflow, and leave clear instructions for the people responsible for it.
